1. Several claims exceed what the supplied notes establish.

   **A universal Malaysian method.** Plan §1 says, “A mean mark, which no quality office reports on its own.” The course record note §2 says, “Threshold practice varies,” and §1 describes the UTHM report as “an example, not a national template.” These examples do not support the universal claim or the heading “Attainment, the way quality offices report it.” Configurable institutional practice is supported. A single national reporting method is not.

   **Numerical mapping weights.** Plan §8.1 specifies “H, M and L as 3, 2 and 1.” The course record note describes UTHM as “using the average approach (every semester)” and the Derouich paper as using “PLO as the weighted sum of CLOs.” Neither quoted description establishes that H, M and L should become those numerical weights. That conversion is an invented product convention, not a researched institutional rule.

   **Collection priorities.** Plan §7.2 says its three collection routes appear “in the order the research recommends.” The technical note instead makes “a DOCX export shaped for Microsoft Forms Quick Import” primary, alongside Google Forms, and describes the paste token as “the last resort.” The plan puts the booth collector first, paste second and campus exports third. That is a different product decision and should not be attributed to the recommendation.

   **The response floor.** Plan §3.1 attributes “5 responses” to “The McGill and McLennan practice.” The technical note §5 describes McGill as requiring “a minimum of five responses for classes of 5 to 11, then 40, 35, 30 and 25 percent by size.” McLennan supports a fixed five response example. McGill does not support treating five as its rule for every class, including the proposed class of 38.

   **Release before grades.** Plan §7.3 requires “comments locked until the coordinator records” that grades are released. The technical note’s McGill policy says “nothing disclosed to the instructor before final grades are processed.” The course record note likewise requires “results released to the lecturer only after grades.” The technical note’s own final recommendation inconsistently narrows this to a comment lock. The plan follows that weaker recommendation without resolving its conflict with the cited policies.

   **Nulty as a standard.** Plan §7.3 calls this “Nulty's standard as the comparison.” The course record note reports, through Ahmad, that “Nulty (2008) recommends about 58% for classes under 20 and 35% over 50.” This is a secondary summary, not an established acceptance standard for the sample class of 38. Neither quoted size category covers that class. The historical Monash response rate is also an observation, not a validity threshold.

   **Judging rules and score effects.** Plan §2 states, “A judge runs three tests in ten minutes,” and says “a named weakness raises scores.” The judge note says, “The finalist pack settles the timings; no rubric was found,” and reports one judge’s observation that “a named weakness raised scores.” The plan generalises an anecdote and a presentation heuristic into expectations about this competition that the notes do not establish.

2. The plan conflicts with binding rules and leaves several internal gates inconsistent.

   **Model authorship directly contradicts the operating brief and as built record.** The operating brief prohibits model authored text “inside shipped artefacts before the pilot.” The as built record specifies “an editable teaching pack with no model text.” Plan §0.2.4 calls this a runtime restriction and commissions model authored course records, slides, assessments and survey responses as shipped fixtures. The original prohibition contains no runtime qualification. Reviewing the fixtures or calling them data does not change their authorship. The proposed owner confirmation would authorise a policy change; it cannot establish that the existing rule remains unchanged.

   **The colour exclusion changes a binding rule.** The operating brief requires colours and type to come from `src/theme/tokens.ts`. D0 deliberately introduces partner colours outside that source and excludes their directory from the checker. This is a policy exception. Passing the altered checker would not demonstrate compliance with the unchanged rule.

   **The slide specification does not fulfil the labelling rule.** The operating brief requires honesty labels on every surface. The plan additionally requires the demo line wherever the UTP mark appears. D2 specifies the mark on content slides but specifies the honesty line only on the closing master. An isolated content slide would therefore have institutional branding without the required disclosure unless an additional requirement is supplied.

   **The recording gate conflicts with earlier acceptance work.** Section 0.2 prohibits every render and export run before approval. D0 rasterises marks, D1 tests an exported ZIP, and D2 generates fourteen packs for inspection. The command list does not resolve whether these verification exports are exempt. The plan cannot require them before D9 while retaining an unqualified prohibition.

   **Capture approval and institutional branding approval are separate gates.** D9 captures branded app screens and places screenshots into the deck and poster. Section 18 separately restricts the mark on those surfaces and in the film. A general instruction to record would not satisfy the university confirmation requirement. The D9 prompt does not check that distinction.

   **The host instructions conflict internally.** Section 0.2 limits speech to welcome, empty states, completions and recap, and excludes provenance. D6 requires explanatory speech at ten stops, including quotations, counts and synthetic results. It does not explain how those lines remain within the narrower rule.

   The supplied as built extract is insufficient to verify most of §1’s detailed claims about current code. Those claims should remain unverified rather than being treated as confirmed by this review.

3. The schedule fails its own dependency order before implementation speed is considered.

   Tier 2 depends on Tier 1 being green on Sunday night, but Tier 1 includes D8, scheduled for Monday. Running D3 and D4 after that gate contradicts their weekend slots. The unconfirmed flight may shorten the available window further.

   **D0 and D1, Friday.** D1’s complete course ZIP depends on D2’s packs and lab sheets, D4’s OBE report, and D7’s quiz and rubric exports. It cannot meet that acceptance before those packages exist. Cut the complete bundle promise first. A manifest can identify unavailable components, but that is a narrower deliverable.

   **D2, Friday and Saturday.** Fourteen sessions require 84 to 126 authored slides with notes, ten lab sheets, a project brief, masters, a second renderer for previews, fourteen generated files and subject review. This cannot credibly share the stated window with the surrounding schema, course screen and comparison work. Cut the fourteen week content promise first, retaining the material needed to inspect the demonstrated revision. Automated text production does not complete subject review.

   **D5, Saturday.** This combines several diff algorithms, historical data handling, structural slide highlighting, a swipe interface, a scrubber, charts, counters and accessibility checks across several screens. Cut slide swipe, onion skin, the scrubber and counters first. The wording diff, hours table and decision link carry the essential claim.

   **D6, Sunday.** A new guided runner, ten stops, workspace restoration, phone layouts, a printable recap, a programme wall and a kiosk are several features, not one page. Cut the programme wall, kiosk and animated claim counters first. Their removal reduces Tier 1 itself, which cutting later tiers does not accomplish.

   **D3, Saturday and Sunday.** Two instruments, a phone page, a network collector, paste codes, DOCX generation, three import formats, release controls and privacy checks do not fit this shared window as verified collection infrastructure. Cut DOCX export, additional import formats and secondary collection paths first. Cutting access controls or response integrity would leave an unsafe demonstration of the claimed survey process.

   **D4, Sunday.** The direct table is much smaller than the full package. The UAEU method requires data the proposed intake does not retain, while the PLO calculation requires an unresolved measurement policy. Cut those two options first. More coding time cannot recover information already discarded at intake.

   **D7, Monday.** This is the first whole package to cut. Semantic item checks, three question types, two export standards and LMS compatibility exceed the stated verification. However, cutting D7 also removes outputs already promised by D1 and D2. Those dependencies prevent it from being genuinely optional under the present acceptance lines.

   **D8 and D9.** D8 is treated as documentation despite adding tools, extending integration checks and repeating full verification after each tier. Cut the new door tools before cutting regression checks. D9’s hour is explicitly machine time; it supplies no allowance for visual corrections, actual player testing or another capture after a fault. Cut secondary media outputs before compressing inspection. The outstanding Claude Desktop, NVDA and actual offline checks in the as built record also remain outstanding.

4. A quality office would challenge the course record, survey process and attainment definitions on the following grounds.

   **The accreditation claim exceeds the file.** The plan does not establish which accreditation framework governs the demo programme. Its engineering checklist explicitly requires evidence that §4.5 excludes, including graded samples and complete examination evidence. Naming those omissions in a manifest documents an incomplete demonstration file. It does not establish accreditation depth. The fallback to only the 2013 and 2014 references also fails the plan’s own recent reference requirement, although retaining those books alongside current references is a separate matter.

   **The CLO5 taxonomy change is not defensible as written.** Both versions require students to “design and present.” The technical note places “design” under Create. Changing static output to interactive output does not by itself explain a change from Apply to Create. CLO3, CLO4 and CLO5 also contain multiple assessed actions despite the stated requirement for one measurable verb.

   **The baseline already contains much of the advertised change.** It already teaches interaction, two dashboard weeks and a dashboard project. The before and after cannot imply that dashboards or interactive work were absent. The plan needs evidence that the proposed hours, assessment and materials actually change, beyond changing CLO wording.

   **The table of specifications cannot be computed from the proposed fields.** A component weight, a list of CLOs and a list of Bloom levels do not allocate marks among those CLOs and levels. The 25% project and 40% examination are especially ambiguous. Counting the full component against every linked CLO would duplicate weight. Equal division would introduce an unstated assessment decision.

   **The rubric cannot yet support the promised marking arithmetic.** Its model contains criteria and performance descriptions but no criterion weights, score values or allocation to CLOs. It does not explicitly assess CLO4’s usability evidence and evaluation of competing designs. A team project also needs evidence supporting individual CLO attainment; a common team mark does not establish each member’s performance.

   **The SLT checks establish totals without establishing workload.** One weekly topic with one kind and an hours value does not explain the separate lecture, lab and assessment allocations. The assessment work breakdown omits the critique essay. It is unclear whether assessed lab work, quizzes and presentations are already counted within contact time. Moving two lab hours also requires a changed weekly allocation, not merely a total that remains 56.

   **Item lint is being given assessment authority it cannot establish.** Surface rules can detect prohibited phrases or option counts. They cannot establish one defensible answer, plausible misconceptions, absence of semantic clues or the claimed cognitive demand. Inserting a Bloom verb into a stem does not create an assessment at that level. A lint pass is insufficient grounds for approving an item for teaching or examination use.

   **Export checks stop short of their claims.** Well formed XML does not establish a valid QTI package or a successful Moodle import. The technical note explicitly recommends schema validation and an actual Moodle import; D7 substitutes a parser check. PPTX checks for titles, notes and font size likewise omit alt text and do not establish sensible reading order or absence of clipped content.

   **The proposed SSRT is a new questionnaire.** Selecting and rewriting twelve items from SEEQ dimensions does not establish equivalence to SEEQ or to UTP’s undisclosed instrument. The adapted label identifies its origin but supplies no validation. The response anchors, handling of unanswered items and treatment of questions a respondent cannot assess also need definition before means and target shares have a stable interpretation.

   **The collection design does not establish anonymity or enforce release restrictions.** Paste codes returned through email or an LMS can identify their sender. Comments can identify students even after name columns are discarded. The proposed API uses the same token in submission and response retrieval routes, with no separate reader authorisation specified. A display floor and a comment lock would not protect raw responses exposed through that route. Linking UTP’s general notice does not establish who operates this prototype or governs its response handling.

   **Booth participants are not the course cohort.** Judges provide real answers about a demonstration, not student evidence about teaching they received. Marking those records `sample: false` without explicit exclusion from course reports risks counting booth activity as educational evidence. The plan also lacks a defined approach to repeated submissions, duplicate imports and eligible respondents. Five requests are not necessarily five distinct responses.

   **The threshold counts become stale when settings change.** Intake stores `atOrAbove` but does not specify the threshold and policy version attached to that count. Changing the institution setting could relabel an old count as if it had been computed at the new threshold. Ten histogram bins cannot recover the count at an arbitrary new threshold, such as 57. Historical reports also need their original method and KPI preserved.

   **The attainment denominator is unresolved.** The plan does not define how missing marks, absence, withdrawal or repeat attempts affect n, N and the KPI. Dividing successful students by valid marks and dividing them by enrolment answer different questions. Displaying n of N does not settle which denominator the attainment share uses.

   **The UAEU option is not computable from the retained data.** That method requires attainment for each assessment tool, its maximum and its weight. Consider two equally weighted tools marked out of 100. Two students scoring `(70, 70)` each produce 100% tool attainment. Students scoring `(100, 40)` and `(40, 100)` produce 50% tool attainment. Both cases produce identical final CLO marks of 70 for both students. The proposed CLO summaries cannot distinguish them.

   **The PLO result is an unsupported index.** A weighted average of CLO pass shares is not a measured share of students attaining a PLO. H, M and L express mapping strength, but the plan has not justified treating them as equally spaced numerical quantities. Calling the result a course contribution limits its scope without validating its weights or establishing that comparison with 60% reproduces the cited institutional method.

   **The direct and indirect gap needs a defined unit.** A direct percentage cannot be subtracted from a mean on a scale of 1 to 5. Comparing two shares produces percentage points, but achievement above 55 and confidence at 4 or higher remain different constructs. Different respondent coverage further limits interpretation. Such a gap cannot establish individual miscalibration or learning gain.

   **Wilson intervals need an inference target.** If all 38 eligible marks are observed, the recorded cohort proportion is known. An interval would concern a model for some wider population or repeated process, not uncertainty about the filed count itself. It does not account for missing marks, survey selection or shared team scores. The separately referenced statistics note was not supplied, so its treatment of these issues cannot be checked here.

   **Historical comparisons need more than matching CLO identifiers.** CLO5 changes meaning, and the plan also changes teaching and potentially assessment. Prior and current pass shares are therefore not automatically comparable measures. The plan does not specify immutable snapshots of the assessment allocation, rubric, survey wording, threshold policy and teaching pack used for each term. A sentence denying learning gain does not resolve those measurement differences.
